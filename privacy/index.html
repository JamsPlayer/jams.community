Jams — Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 29, 2026 · Last updated: October 29, 2026

This policy covers the Jams apps: Jams Player, Personal Soundtrack, and JamStand (together, "the Apps"), on desktop and mobile. The Apps are provided by JAMS ECOSYSTEM LLC, a Michigan limited liability company ("Jams", "we", "us"). Contact: contact@jams.community.

At a glance

  • We run no servers that hold your data. Your library, history, settings and most of what the Apps learn about you are stored on your own device. We do not have an account database, and we cannot look you up.
  • But some things do leave your device, and some are public. The Apps talk to other people's devices over a peer-to-peer network called x0x, and store some files on a permanent public network called Autonomi. Some of what the Apps send is visible to other users and to artists, linked to your app identity (a pseudonym, explained below). Section 3 lists each one.
  • Some sharing has no off switch today. In Jams Player, what you are playing right now and the tracks you like are broadcast to the network, and artists receive a message each time you finish one of their tracks. There is currently no setting to turn these off.
  • We don't sell your personal information, don't share it for advertising, and have no ads or ad trackers.
  • Payments are public. Payments happen on a public blockchain, where anyone can see which wallet paid which artist.
  • Research sharing is opt-in, adults only, and off unless you turn it on.

1. Your app identity

When you first open an App, it creates a cryptographic identity on your device (an "agent id" and a four-word address). There are no usernames or passwords. This identity is a pseudonym: it doesn't contain your name, but it stays the same over time and anything signed or sent with it can be linked together. Treat it as personal information. If you choose a display name or avatar, they are attached to this identity and visible to others.

2. What stays on your device

These are stored only on your device, and we never receive them:

  • your music library, file paths, playlists, stations and listening history;
  • your app settings and preferences;
  • your date of birth, which you enter at first launch. It is stored in a plain (unencrypted) file on your device and used to apply age rules (Section 10) and, if you ask, to find music from years of your life;
  • in Personal Soundtrack, the moods you pick and the context the App derives (time, weather, rough location, and, if you connect a wearable, sleep and recovery data). Raw wearable and mood data never leave your device, except for the recommendation list described in Section 3.7;
  • app usage records (which features you use and how long things take). They stay on your device. If you link your own devices together, a redacted copy syncs between them (Section 3.4). We do not receive them, and the Apps contain no analytics or advertising SDK.

3. What leaves your device, and who can see it

3.1 Network connections

To work at all, the Apps connect to the x0x and Autonomi networks. Those connections reveal your device's IP address to the network's starting nodes (operated by third parties, not by us) and to the other devices you connect to. We do not operate any of these nodes.

3.3 Messages to artists and other users

  • When you finish a track, Jams Player and Personal Soundtrack send the artist a message with your app identity, the track, when you played it, for how long, and the listening context. Jams Player re-sends the last 7 days of these when it starts, in case some were missed. There is no setting to turn this off today.
  • Weekly listening summary. Jams Player sends each artist you listen to a weekly summary: how often you played them, when you first played them, and how many other artists you listened to. This is on by default; you can turn it off in Settings → Artist support ("Count my listens toward artist verification"). It is used to help artists get verified.
  • Play receipts. When you play a track published on the network, Jams Player asks a small group of other Jams Player users to co-sign a receipt of that play. The receipt includes your app identity, the track and the play time. Jams Player also lists your device as available to co-sign other people's receipts. There is no setting in the app to turn this off today.
  • Things you choose to send: song and playlist shares, notes, invites and gifts go to the person you pick.

3.4 Your own linked devices

If you link your devices, they sync your library index, collections, covers, device history and a redacted copy of your usage records, directly and only between your devices.

3.5 Play accounting

Jams Player is built to send monthly play tallies that are used to pay artists and curators. Each tally is a per-month, per-artist count, protected with a rotating salt, and it doesn't include your app identity. For playlists or stations you curate, it includes a one-way code derived from your identity. This is part of how the service works and is on for everyone. Today it is inactive: no recipient for these tallies has been configured, so nothing is sent.

3.6 Research (opt-in, adults only)

If you are 18 or older and turn on research sharing, the Apps send anonymized listening events to a research aggregator. Before anything is sent, the events are anonymized on your device. Wearable and biometric data are never included. Turning research off stops sending and deletes anything still waiting to be sent. Today no aggregator is configured, so nothing is sent even when research is on.

3.7 Personal Soundtrack recommendations to your own device

If you set it up, Personal Soundtrack can send a ranked list of recommendations (which may reflect your sleep or recovery data) to another device you name.

3.9 Autonomi (permanent public storage)

Anything written to Autonomi is permanent, and we cannot delete it.

  • Jams Player offload (if you use it): your audio is encrypted on your device before upload. A small signed pointer record, which includes your app identity and where the archive is stored, is public.
  • JamStand publishing (artists): see Section 5.

3.10 Payments and funding

  • Payments. Payments are made from a wallet on your own device. We never hold your funds. A payment authorization (your wallet address, the artist's wallet address, the amount and a signature) goes to a payment facilitator (x402, operated by a third party), which submits it to the Base public blockchain. Blockchain transactions are public and permanent: anyone can see which wallet paid which artist wallet, and when. If your wallet can be linked to you, so can the artists you pay. Today payments run only on a test network with test funds.
  • Adding funds (Transak). If you add funds, the App sends Transak your wallet address, the amount and an order reference, and opens Transak's checkout in your browser. Transak, not Jams, collects any identity verification and payment details under its own privacy policy. The App keeps a local record of the order. Adults only.

3.11 Crash reports

If an App crashes, it can prepare a report: app, version, operating system, the error message and a technical trace with file paths, addresses, IP addresses and email addresses removed, plus a short fragment of your app identity's signature. Nothing is sent unless you click to submit it. Submitting opens a public issue page on GitHub in your browser. Anyone can read reports submitted there.

On iPhone, if you allow Apple to share diagnostics with developers, Apple may also share crash diagnostics with us through Apple's systems.

3.12 App stores and TestFlight

Apple and Google collect information when you download, install or test the Apps (for TestFlight, this includes your email and any feedback you send) under their own privacy policies. We receive only what their developer tools show us.

4. How we use information

We use the information described above only to run the Apps' features, to connect artists with listeners, to process payments you authorise, to fix crashes you report, and to comply with law. We don't use it for advertising, we don't build advertising profiles, and we don't sell it.

5. Artists using JamStand

When you publish in JamStand, what you publish is public and permanent:

  • your artist card (sent to the x0x network), including the city, state and country you enter, follower and listener counts, play counts, and your app identities;
  • your track records and files on Autonomi, including audio, artwork and lyrics, plus the rights details you enter: songwriter names, IPI/CAE numbers, performing-rights and mechanical-rights organisations, and ISWC and ISRC codes, and, where enabled, your payout wallet address. Anyone can then see payments to that address on the blockchain.

If you enter co-writers' details, they are published too. Only enter details you are entitled to publish. Your registration details are also stored on your device in a plain (unencrypted) file.

6. When we share information

We don't sell or rent personal information, and don't share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. Because we don't hold a central copy of your data, there is usually nothing for us to disclose. If we ever do hold information about you (for example, an email you send us), we may disclose it only (a) to service providers acting for us under confidentiality obligations, (b) to comply with law or valid legal process, (c) to protect users, the public or our rights, or (d) in a merger or sale of the business, with notice as the law requires.

Physical orders are not yet available.

7. How long information is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it or uninstall the App.
  • Deleting your data in Jams Player (desktop): "Delete my data" turns research off, then removes the App's local stores. It keeps one private backup copy on your device in case you deleted by mistake; the deletion report tells you where it is, and you can remove it yourself. It does not remove anything already sent to other devices or written to Autonomi, or the identity keys held in your system keychain. Jams Player mobile, Personal Soundtrack and JamStand have no delete or export feature yet.
  • Sent to other users or artists, broadcast to x0x, written to Autonomi, or recorded on a blockchain: kept by those systems and people. We cannot delete it.
  • Third-party services: under their own policies.
  • Emails you send us: kept only as long as needed to answer you and to keep a record of your request, then deleted.

8. Security

The Apps are designed so that most data never leaves your device. Messages are signed, offloaded audio is encrypted before upload, and wallet keys stay on your device. Some information is stored on your device unencrypted, including your date of birth, JamStand registration details, and wearable login tokens (protected only by your device's file permissions). No system is perfectly secure. Protect your device, and back up your keys and recovery information.

9. Do Not Track and third-party tracking

The Apps contain no advertising or analytics trackers, and we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites. The Apps don't respond to browser "Do Not Track" signals.

Third parties may still collect information about you across sites and services. Requests the Apps make to the services in Section 3.8 reveal your IP address and what is being looked up, and those services may log them. In particular, many podcast episode links pass through podcast measurement services (for example Podtrac or Chartable) that count downloads across publishers. Artwork can also be loaded from the website that hosts it. Each of these handles data under its own policy.

10. Children and teens

  • The Apps are not directed to children. Each App asks for your birth date the first time you open it. If you enter a birth date under 13, the App blocks use. Today, deleting Jams Player's data also clears this block; we are fixing that.
  • If you are 13 to 17, the App works, but these are turned off: buying, subscribing, gifting, pledging, backing campaigns and adding funds; research sharing; and in Personal Soundtrack, wearable and biometric features. Today, per-play payment settlement is not separately age-gated (a 13–17 user can't add funds, but settlement itself does not check age), and the Personal Soundtrack mobile demo path skips the adult check when you connect a wearable; we are fixing both.
  • The Apps do not yet read the age range that Apple or Google provides, which Texas law requires; we are adding that.
  • If you believe a child under 13 has sent us information, contact us and we will delete what we hold.

11. Your rights

Because your data is on your device, you can see and delete most of it yourself, within the limits described in Section 7. Depending on where you live, you may also have rights to know, access, correct, delete or port the personal information a business holds about you, to opt out of its sale, sharing or targeted advertising (we do none of these), and not to be discriminated against for using these rights. To make a request, email contact@jams.community. In most cases we hold no data about you to return.

12. Users outside the United States

The Apps are intended for users in the United States. If you use them from elsewhere, the networks they connect to are global, and other users and network nodes may be in any country.
